M.Sc. thesis — Instant Visualization of Organic Matter Transformation in the Ocean

MARUM · BIOM · MPI Marine Microbiology · 10/2024–12/2025 · defended 23.03.2026

How fast do bacteria dismantle sinking particles, and what decides the rate?

  • Synthesised calcium-alginate microparticles (124±49 µm) and ran five degradation experiments (ADE I–V) on six marine Vibrio strains by time-lapse microscopy.
  • Showed phosphate is the switch: at 0.2 mM, enzyme-releasing strains removed 2.1× more particle volume (82.5% vs 39.3%; Mann–Whitney U=959, P=0.0005; n>300). At zero phosphate the advantage disappears.
  • Traced the effect to a single alginate-lyase gene (PL7G) rather than the phenotype label: in co-culture the 2.2× monoculture advantage collapses to 1.05×.

Methods — EVOS time-lapse microscopy · ImageJ particle sizing (Feret/ESD) · Hungarian-algorithm particle tracking · 96-well OD₆₀₀ growth kinetics · custom minimal-media design · all statistics & figures in Python 3.11 (NumPy, SciPy, pandas, matplotlib, seaborn).
