Research experience

M.Sc. thesis — Instant Visualization of Organic Matter Transformation in the Ocean
MARUM (Hinrichs Lab) · MPI for Marine Microbiology, Bridge Group Marine Glycobiology · BIOM (Hehemann Lab) · 10/2024 – 12/2025, defended 23.03.2026 · within the DFG framework TRR 420 CONCENTRATE · Guest Scientist, AG Hehemann; Research Student, MPI Bremen
  • Designed and ran five particle-degradation experiments (ADE I–V) on six marine Vibrio strains — mono- and co-culture, biological triplicates, incubations to 590 h — tracking ~300 individually matched particles per run. Two degradation phenotypes separated cleanly: broadcasters removed 78.1 ± 17.6 % of particle volume against aggregators’ 50.5 ± 24.2 %.
  • Built the system from scratch: calcium-alginate microparticles (124 ± 49 µm) by emulsification/internal gelation, and a custom Marine Minimal Medium family isolating one nutrient per experiment — PO₄³⁻ 0–0.4 mM, NH₄⁺ 0–20 mM, casamino acids 0–1.0 g L⁻¹.
  • Central measured result: phosphate acts as a specific metabolic switch — at 0.2 mM the broadcaster phenotype removed 2.1× more particle volume (median 82.5 % vs 39.3 %, n > 300 particles); the advantage vanishes under phosphate depletion, and neither ammonium nor amino acids reproduce it. In co-culture the 2.2× advantage of the strongest degrader collapses to 1.05× — traced to one alginate-lyase gene (PL7G), not the phenotype label.
  • Built the whole particle-imaging-to-rate pipeline in Python 3.11: time-lapse imaging (EVOS FL Auto) → ImageJ Feret/ESD sizing → particle volume → daily carbon degradation rate → carbon loss per metre, with Hungarian-algorithm particle matching across timepoints. A settling model built on the measured rates predicts Martin exponents of 0.95 vs 0.78 — ~11 % vs ~17 % of particle carbon surviving to 1,000 m.
¹³C stable-isotope probing and diffusive-flux geochemistry — Helgoland Mud Area (North Sea)
Biogeochemistry Projects, MARUM / AWI · 2022 · gravity core HE595 GC-11, 452 cm, shore-based · analytical support M. Elvert, V. Heuer, L. Wörmer; porewater section reviewed by Prof. Dr. S. Kasten
  • Designed and ran ¹³C-sodium-acetate incubations in three arms — 48 h/28 °C, 120 h/28 °C, 120 h/8 °C plus a frozen t₀ control — on sediments from four depths spanning the sulfate-reduction, sulfate–methane-transition and methanogenic zones, with anaerobic handling throughout.
  • Traced the label into individual fatty acids by GC-C-IRMS (strongest uptake in C16:1ω7 and C16:1ω5) and showed bacterial communities respond to elevated temperature faster than archaeal communities across the redox zonation; characterised the archaeal community by lipid biomarkers, where the OH-archaeol/archaeol ratio resolves an ANME-1 → ANME-2 shift across the sulfate–methane transition — anaerobic methane oxidation read from intact lipids.
  • Extracted porewater by rhizone sampling (0.1 µm), measured nutrients and DIC on a QuAAtro39 segmented-flow analyser, and converted the sulfate gradient into a diffusive flux by Fick’s first law with tortuosity correction (φ = 0.6, θ² = 2.02): J = −0.219 mol m⁻² yr⁻¹, read as a measure of microbial sulfate reduction. Documented the redox cascade after oxygen exposure of reduced sediment — sulfide re-oxidation, Fe and Mn re-precipitation, phosphate re-adsorption onto fresh oxides: a rapid redox shift followed through the porewater.

Chemical imaging of a millimetre-scale redox gradient — hypersaline microbial mat
