# Output 4 — Structural notes and the language-certificate question

Retrieved 2026-09-07 throughout. Every URL cited was opened on that date (some through a text-reader proxy where the host blocks automated fetches; the canonical URL is cited). "Not verified" means no page carrying the figure could be opened in this pass.

## (a) How PhD funding actually works, region by region

### 1. Germany
A German PhD is normally a **job**: a TV-L E13 contract at 65–75 % (Helmholtz, Max Planck, DFG Graduiertenkollegs, ERC teams, university projects), taxed, with pension, unemployment and health insurance; there are no tuition fees, only a semester contribution of roughly EUR 300–400. The alternative is a **tax-free stipend** from one of the 13 Begabtenförderungswerke at the joint BMFTR rate: "Ab dem Wintersemester 2025/2026 erhalten Stipendiatinnen und Stipendiaten monatlich 1.650 Euro. Zusätzlich ... eine monatliche Forschungskostenpauschale von 100 Euro" (https://www.bmftr.bund.de/DE/Forschung/Wissenschaftssystem/WissenschaftlicheKarriere/Promotionsstipendium/promotionsstipendium.html), i.e. EUR 1,750/month net, but with no pension accrual and only a 50 % health-insurance subsidy capped at EUR 100. The exact net figure of a 65 % E13 contract was not verified on any page opened in this pass; it is broadly comparable to the stipend in cash but far better in social cover. Consequence: search for **Stellen / positions** (institute job pages, EURAXESS, DFG GRK cohorts) first, and treat the stipends as a portable add-on you bring to a supervisor. None of the foundations counts time since a *first* doctoral enrolment; they count time since the last degree (Studienstiftung 4 years 3 months; KAS 5 years) or progress on the current project.

### 2. Rest of the EU and associated countries (Netherlands, Nordics, Switzerland, Austria, France)
Same model as Germany, more uniform: the doctoral candidate is a salaried employee, no fees, no scholarship round, no proposal, no prior-enrolment clause found anywhere. Verified gross figures: Netherlands scale P EUR 3,059–3,881/month + 8 % holiday + 8.3 % year-end (https://www.staff.universiteitleiden.nl/human-resources/conditions-of-employment-salary-and-allowance/salary/salary-scales-and-periodic-rise); Norway NOK 568,700/year (https://www.jobbnorge.no/en/available-jobs/joblisting/pdf/293286); Denmark DKK 37,313.75/month including pension at entry (https://phd.arts.au.dk/fileadmin/phd.arts.au.dk/ARTS/Rules_and_forms/Loenoversigt_UK.pdf); Sweden SEK 32,900/month from 1 Oct 2026 (https://medarbetare.su.se/en/employment/my-employment/salary-and-compensation/salary-for-doctoral-students); Switzerland (EPFL) CHF 55,225/year (https://www.epfl.ch/campus/services/human-resources/basic-starting-salary-of-doctoral-assistants-and-postdocs); Austria (FWF) EUR 2,832.10/month at 30 h/week (https://www.fwf.ac.at/en/funding/steps-to-your-fwf-project/further-information/personnel-costs); France EUR 2,300 gross/month from 1 Jan 2026 by arrêté (https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000046820745). All are taxed at national rates; net figures were not verified. The fellowship-based fringe (Belgium FWO, Portugal FCT, Ireland, Italy) is where seniority caps and prior-funding clauses appear — FWO's "maximum of 18 months of scientific seniority" is the sharpest hazard for him in Europe. MSCA Doctoral Networks are employment too, but the mobility rule closes German-hosted nodes to a Bremen resident and opens every other country's nodes.

### 3. United States
No portable national scholarship is open to him: NSF GRFP, Hertz, DOE CSGF, ARCS, NOAA Foster, NDSEG, AMS, Sea Grant and SMART are all citizen/PR-only (Output 3). The money is a **departmental package attached to admission**: a Research or Teaching Assistantship (a part-time employment appointment, W-2 wage) or internal fellowship, plus tuition remission and a health-insurance plan or subsidy, on identical terms for internationals. Verified stipends run from USD 30,000 (Texas A&M pre-candidacy) through USD 37,500 (Scripps year 1), ~USD 42,000–43,600 (URI; UW), USD 46,500 (Caltech) to USD 49,614 (MIT–WHOI). Three departments guarantee funding in writing (Penn State, UW, Caltech); URI and Oregon State explicitly do not. Tax: the assistantship wage is federally taxable (plus state tax outside TX/WA/FL); the US–Germany treaty exempts only USD 9,000/year of wages and only if the stay is "not more than 4 years", and "If the individual's visit exceeds 4 years, this exemption is lost for the entire visit" (IRS Publication 901, Germany, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p901) — so for a 5–6-year PhD the treaty is a non-event; FICA is exempt for the first five calendar years on F-1/J-1 (https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-student-liability-for-social-security-and-medicare-taxes). Budget roughly USD 3,000–6,000/year of tax on a USD 40,000 stipend (estimate, not a sourced figure). Search for **advisers and programmes**, not scholarships; the deadline wall is 1–15 December 2026.

### 4. Canada
A **funding package** assembled by the department from "internal or external awards, teaching-related work, research assistantships, and graduate academic assistantships" (UBC minimum funding policy, https://www.grad.ubc.ca/awards/minimum-funding-policy-phd-students), not an employment contract. UBC guarantees CAD 40,000/year for four years to domestic and international PhD students from September 2026, but tuition (CAD 10,081.65 + ~1,203 fees) is deducted first, leaving ~CAD 28,700–29,000 (~CAD 2,390/month). McGill EPS states a CAD 25,000 take-home with the international differential neutralised. Award income is tax-free for a full-time student ("generally not taxable if you received them for your enrolment in a program for which you are considered a full-time qualifying student", CRA line 13010, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-13000-other-income/line-13010-scholarships-fellowships-bursaries-artists-project-grants-awards.html); TA/RA wages are taxed. The tri-agency award (CGRS D, CAD 40,000) is open to internationals only after registration at a Canadian institution and capped at 15 % of awards, so the route is two-step: institutional package first, CGRS D in year 1–2. Vanier and NSERC PGS-D/CGS-D no longer exist.

### 5. Australia
A **tax-free scholarship**, not employment: "Full-time scholarships are considered by the Australian Taxation Office to be exempt income, and are therefore not taxed" (UTAS, https://www.utas.edu.au/research/degrees/scholarships-and-fees). A funded international PhD is three separate awards that must all land: an RTP/university stipend (Commonwealth base AUD 34,315 in 2026, AUD 35,550 in 2027; actual rates UTAS 34,315 · JCU 37,500 · UNSW 39,206 · UWA 39,253 · Melbourne 39,500 · ANU 40,475 · Sydney 44,293), an RTP Fees Offset to extinguish international tuition of roughly AUD 35,000–50,000/year, and OSHC (health cover, ~AUD 4,500–4,700 single). The scarce component is the fee offset: "A HEP must only spend up to a maximum of 10 per cent of its RTP Grant Amount in a Grant Year on the provision of RTP Scholarships to overseas students" and it must be awarded "as a result of a competitive selection process" (Guidelines 2025 s.15(2), s.17(3), https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01474/asmade/2025-12-02/text/original/word). UTAS excludes OSHC and visa costs from both TGRS and RTP; Sydney (RTP route), Melbourne, ANU, UWA and UNSW RTPI bundle OSHC. Net monthly: roughly AUD 2,860–3,700 tax-free. Federally there is no prior-doctoral-enrolment bar (Department FAQs 9, 10, 13); universities may prioritise "students undertaking a HDR for the first time".

### 6. New Zealand
Also a **tax-free scholarship** model (Inland Revenue IS 15/01, s CW 36: "Certain scholarships and bursaries are exempt income under s CW 36", https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/interpretation-statements/is-1501-income-tax-tax-exempt-scholarships-and-bursaries-s-cw-36), with one structural difference that changes the maths: international PhD students pay **domestic** tuition — "If you are a new international PhD student, you will pay the same annual tuition fee as New Zealand PhD students. The 2026 PhD tuition fee is NZ$8,847.60 per annum", conditional on residing in NZ with at most 12 months of research overseas (https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/study/fees-and-money-matters/tuition-fees/doctoral-fees.html; Waikato NZ$7,567–8,037; VUW confirms the policy). So the fee gap that dominates the UK/Australian calculation largely disappears and the stipend is the whole question. Verified 2026 stipends: Auckland NZ$35,468 (42 months, + fees + SSF + single-student health insurance), Otago NZ$34,128, Waikato NZ$33,000, Canterbury NZ$32,650, VUW NZ$32,000 — roughly NZ$2,670–2,960/month tax-free; only Auckland covers the international health-insurance charge. There is no national scheme (NZIDRS "is not currently being offered"). A partner of a level-10 (PhD) student gets an open work visa (NZD 1,630; https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-student-work-visa/) and PhD graduates get up to 3 years' open post-study work rights from thesis submission (https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/post-study-work-visa/).

### 7. United Kingdom
A **studentship**: a tax-free stipend with no employment contract, pension or social insurance. UKRI minimum GBP 21,805/year from 1 October 2026 (GBP 23,805 with London weighting), i.e. ~GBP 1,817/month outside London, plus home-rate fees of GBP 5,238 (https://www.ukri.org/apply-for-funding/studentships-and-doctoral-training/get-a-studentship-to-fund-your-doctorate/). International students are eligible for UKRI studentships, capped at "a maximum of 30% of the individuals funded by UKRI per cohort per training grant" (https://www.ukri.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ESRC-020323-InternationalEligibilityImplementationGuidance-TrainingGrantHolders.pdf; still current per the ICASE terms page), and UKRI money may not pay the international fee difference: "Training Grant Funds cannot be used to cover the difference between home and overseas fee rates" (https://www.ukri.org/publications/terms-and-conditions-for-training-funding/ukri-training-grants-standard-terms-and-conditions-of-training-grant/). Whether a NERC studentship is fully funded for him therefore depends on the host's waiver: GW4+, ARIES, YES-DTN, E5, ACCE+, QUARTILES and CREATES waive it; CENTA3 does not ("there will likely be a shortfall in fees"); IAPETUS leaves it to the institution; IGNITE does not say. University-funded schemes (Imperial President's GBP 26,500 + full fees, Clarendon, Gates, Harding, Edinburgh Doctoral College, Bristol PGR, Plymouth SERVO) pay the overseas fee with no nationality cap and are the stronger play. No studentship covers the Student visa (GBP 558) or the Immigration Health Surcharge (GBP 776/year for students, paid up-front: https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay) — roughly GBP 3,700 before arrival for a 3.5-year PhD; EU students may be able to claim an IHS refund (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-health-surcharge-for-eu-students-in-the-uk, not opened in detail). There is no England-only NERC scheme; UKRI applies the England fee regulations UK-wide as a legal definition.

## (b) The language question

**Short answer: no. A German UNIcert III (C1) certificate is not accepted as proof of English at any of the UK, US, Canadian, Australian or New Zealand universities checked, and the "prior degree taught in English" waiver does not cover a German-taught M.Sc. with an English thesis. He needs IELTS Academic (target 7.0 overall, no band below 6.5) or TOEFL iBT (≥100 to clear the highest bar, MIT–WHOI), sat by early-to-mid October 2026 for the December/January deadlines.** Only three verified exceptions exist: University of Bremen (and therefore IMPRS MarMic) lists "UNIcert® III" in its C1 column (https://www.uni-bremen.de/en/language-centre-of-the-universities-in-the-land-of-bremen/courses-more/tests-certificates/standardised-language-certificates); Caltech requires no English test and sets "no minimum requirement for the English proficiency exams"; URI's Graduate School accepts "Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR): Required minimum score of B2" in place of TOEFL (UNIcert not named — confirm in writing). Continental salaried posts (NL, NO, DK, SE, CH, AT, FR) ask for "good command of English" without a certificate; UiB may ask for documentation.

### Recommendation
Book a computer-delivered IELTS Academic now, in September 2026, at target 7.0 overall with no band below 6.5: that single score clears every institution above (MIT–WHOI's IELTS 7.0, UCL/TREES 7.0/6.5, UTAS 7.0/6.5, Dalhousie 7.0) and stays valid through any PhD start in 2027 or 2028. Two-week combined lead time (booking plus results) is realistic; three months is the safe planning figure the task assumed. Do not rely on the English-medium-degree waiver anywhere, and do not rely on the UNIcert III outside the University of Bremen. The UNIcert is still useful for MarMic and for continental posts.
