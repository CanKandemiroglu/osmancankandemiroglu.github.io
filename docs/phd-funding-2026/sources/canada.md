Region researcher: CANADA. All entries retrieved **2026-09-07**.

## HEADLINE CORRECTIONS TO THE TASK BRIEF (verified)

1. **The Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships programme no longer exists.** It was terminated after the fall 2024 competition and folded into a new harmonised doctoral award. There is no Vanier deadline to hit, no Vanier nomination process, and the "20 months of doctoral study" rule the task asked me to quote belongs to a dead programme. See section B and the note below on the source problem.
2. **NSERC PGS-D / CGS-D no longer exists either**, and its successor is **NOT** closed to non-Canadians. The replacement — **Canada Graduate Research Scholarship – Doctoral (CGRS D)** — explicitly admits international applicants (capped at 15% of awards) provided they are registered at a Canadian institution by the deadline. The brief's assumption that he is INELIGIBLE for the NSERC doctoral award is **wrong as of the 2025 reform**. He is ineligible only in year 0 (before he is registered), and eligible from the moment he is enrolled.
3. **A new programme exists that is close to purpose-built for this candidate: Canada Impact+ Research Training Awards (CIRTA)** — CAD 40,000/yr × 3 yrs, explicitly to recruit researchers *currently abroad*, **no months-of-study or years-since-degree limit at all**. The 2026 competition's two intakes have both closed (4 Mar 2026, 3 Jun 2026); whether a 2027 intake exists is **not verified** (it was announced as a one-time $133.6M initiative). This is the single highest-value thing to chase with Canadian supervisors.
4. **The 2016–18 Bochum doctoral enrolment is a much smaller problem in Canada than in Europe.** Every months-of-study cap I could open is written against *"the doctoral program … for which you are requesting funding"* or *"your PhD"* at the host institution — i.e. the current programme, not a foreign abandoned one. No Canadian page I opened contains an exclusion for previously having been registered in a doctoral programme elsewhere. Exact wordings and the residual ambiguity are quoted per entry.
5. **The real blocker for this candidate in Canada is the English test, not eligibility.** Every institution below requires IELTS/TOEFL. The waivers are all worded around the *language of instruction of the previous degree* (and at Dalhousie additionally the *country's official language*). His Bremen M.Sc. was a German-taught programme, so **he does not qualify for any waiver I found, and German C1 / UNIcert III is nowhere on any accepted-test list.** He must sit IELTS Academic (7.0 for Dalhousie, 6.5 for UBC) before the January deadlines.

## C. How PhD funding works in Canada

**Structure — package, not contract.** Canada does not use the German TV-L employment model. A funded Canadian PhD is a *funding package* assembled by the department from four components that the UBC minimum-funding policy names verbatim: "any combination of internal or external awards, teaching-related work, research assistantships, and graduate academic assistantships." The student is a student who also does paid TA/RA work, not an employee on a salaried research contract. This matters for him: there is no German-style social-insurance-bearing contract, no automatic pension accrual, and continuation is conditional — McGill EPS states support depends on "satisfactory progress towards your degree as evaluated annually."

**International differential fees, and who absorbs them.** International PhD students are charged more than domestic ones, and the three mechanisms differ sharply. (a) **UBC absorbs it into the guarantee, not on top of it**: the CAD 40,000 floor is gross, and "any funding paid through Workday (i.e., award funding) will first be applied to UBC tuition and fees … before excess funds are paid to the recipient's bank account" — with international PhD tuition at CAD 10,081.65/yr plus ~CAD 1,203.20 in fees, his net is roughly **CAD 28,700–29,000/yr**. (b) **McGill EPS neutralises the differential outright**: "The EPS Department currently provides tuition fee subsidies to out-of-province Canadian students and International students such that the tuition amount paid is identical to that of a Quebec resident", producing a stated "Total take-home" of **CAD 25,000**. (c) **Québec uses a separate exemption instrument**: at Université Laval the international doctoral rate is CAD 100.89/credit tuition *plus* CAD 519.27/credit supplemental fees (≈ CAD 7,442/yr at 12 credits), and the supplemental portion is what the exemption scholarships remove. Meanwhile Dalhousie Oceanography reports that "Students do not typically pay tuition. Instead, support for the students' living and research expenses comes mainly from supervisors' research grants" — i.e. the supervisor's grant, not a university policy, is the guarantee.

**Tax.** Scholarship and fellowship income for a full-time doctoral student is effectively tax-free. CRA, verbatim: "Post-secondary school scholarships, fellowships, and bursaries are generally not taxable if you received them for your enrolment in a program for which you are considered a full-time qualifying student in the tax year, in the immediately preceding tax year, or in the following tax year." Research grants are handled separately — "If you received a research grant, do not report it on line 13010." The exemption is student-specific, not researcher-specific: "An individual undertaking a postdoctoral fellowship is not considered to be enrolled in a qualifying educational program", so the tax advantage disappears the day he finishes. **TA/RA employment income is employment income and is taxed** — so a package's blend matters: an award-heavy CAD 40,000 nets far more than a TA-heavy CAD 40,000. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-13000-other-income/line-13010-scholarships-fellowships-bursaries-artists-project-grants-awards.html

**Typical net monthly.** On the best-verified route (UBC Oceanography, CAD 40,000 gross less ~CAD 11,285 tuition and fees), roughly **CAD 2,390/month net** with most of it tax-exempt. At McGill EPS the department's own stated take-home of CAD 25,000 is **~CAD 2,080/month**. At Memorial, on the published fellowship and assistantship ranges, **~CAD 1,250–1,500/month** — not viable. Against Vancouver and Montreal rents these are modest; UBC's is the only one that clears comfortably.

**Sequencing consequence.** Because the tri-agency award now requires prior registration ("you must be registered in your doctoral program of study at an eligible Canadian institution by the application deadline"), the Canadian route is necessarily two-step: **get admitted on an institutional package first, then win CGRS D in year 1 or 2 to upgrade it.** The exception is CIRTA, which reverses this and recruits from abroad — which is precisely why it is worth an email to CIRTA-BFRIC@nserc-crsng.gc.ca.
