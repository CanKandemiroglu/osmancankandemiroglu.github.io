# USA — PhD funding for a German citizen (M.Sc. Bremen 2026), retrieved 2026-09-07

Region agent: UNITED STATES. All URLs below were opened. Where a page could not be
opened this is stated explicitly. Fulbright from the German side is NOT covered here —
the Germany/EU agent covers it (per brief §3).

**Headline for this region.** In the US the money almost never comes from a portable
national scholarship the candidate can win himself — every US national graduate
fellowship of consequence (NSF GRFP, NDSEG, DOE CSGF, Hertz, ARCS, NOAA Nancy Foster,
AMS, NMFS/Sea Grant, SMART) is closed to non-citizens; verbatim clauses are in §B. The
realistic route is **departmental admission-with-funding**: he applies to the PhD
programme, and the department attaches a Research/Teaching Assistantship or fellowship
that pays a stipend, remits tuition and subsidises health insurance. Several of the ten
departments checked guarantee that package to *all* admitted PhD students including
internationals. The two genuine open-to-internationals competitive awards found are
**NASA FINESST** and the **Link Foundation Ocean Engineering & Instrumentation
Fellowship** — both require him to be already applying to / enrolled at a US university,
so they are second-year moves, not entry tickets.

**Language-test blocker.** He has NO TOEFL/IELTS/Duolingo. Nine of the ten departments
require one; his German-taught Bremen M.Sc. and Turkish-taught Kocaeli B.Sc. do NOT
trigger any waiver (Germany and Türkiye are absent from every exempt-country list
opened: Penn State, Oregon State, URI). Exceptions found: **Caltech** (no test required
for admission, no minimum score) and **URI** (accepts a CEFR B2 certificate in place of
TOEFL — his UNIcert III / C1 may satisfy this; must be confirmed with URI). Everywhere
else a test must be booked and sat **before the December deadline** — this is the single
hardest deadline constraint in this region.

**Prior doctoral enrolment (2016–18 Bochum, no doctorate).** No US department checked
excludes an applicant who *enrolled in* but did not complete a doctorate. US "duplication
of degree" rules bite only on *holding* a doctorate (UCSD, Duke quoted in §A/§D). He
holds none, so he is clear — but transcripts from Bochum for 2016–18 must be disclosed
(every application requires transcripts from **all** institutions attended). The one place
prior enrolment is fatal is NSF GRFP, and he is already excluded there on citizenship.

## C. Notes on how funding works in this region

US PhD funding is not a scholarship you win and carry; with rare exceptions it is a **package attached to admission**, made up of (i) a Research or Teaching Assistantship — which is a *salaried employment appointment*, typically nominally 20 hours per week, paying a monthly stipend and reported on a W-2 — and/or an internal fellowship, plus (ii) **tuition remission** paid by the department to the university, plus (iii) a **health-insurance subsidy or plan**. Non-resident (out-of-state) tuition is normally waived too, and international students are funded on exactly the same basis as domestic students at every one of the ten departments checked; what internationals lose is access to the *federal* fellowship layer (§B), not the departmental layer. Stipends across the ten range from **USD 30,000 p.a.** (Texas A&M, pre-candidacy) through **USD 37,500** (UCSD/Scripps first year), **~USD 42,000–43,600** (URI; UW per its FAQ), **USD 46,500** (Caltech) to **USD 49,614** (MIT–WHOI) — the spread tracks local cost of living, not prestige. Three of the ten put a guarantee in writing (Penn State: "all accepted graduate students are guaranteed funding for their stipend and tuition"; UW: "You will be funded for six years"; Caltech: "guaranteed funding through a Division fellowship for the first five terms ... The Division is committed to funding students throughout their graduate career"), MIT–WHOI and Miami guarantee it in practice ("admitted to the program with full funding"), while **URI and Oregon State explicitly do not** — at URI, "Acceptance into URI-GSO Graduate Programs in Oceanography is separate from funding support", so an offer there can be worth nothing without a named adviser and an identified grant.

**Tax (the structural note the brief asks for).** He would enter on an **F-1** (or J-1) visa. Per the IRS, "Foreign students temporarily present in the U.S. in F-1, J-1, or M-1 status for less than 5 calendar years are generally nonresident aliens (NRA) ... These nonresident alien students are exempt from Social Security Tax and Medicare Tax on wages paid to them for services performed within the U.S." — so no FICA on the RA/TA wage for the first five calendar years (https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-student-liability-for-social-security-and-medicare-taxes, retrieved 2026-09-07). **Federal income tax is a different matter, and the US–Germany treaty (Article 20, Students and Apprentices) helps much less than people assume.** IRS Publication 901, *U.S. Tax Treaties*, Germany, Students and apprentices (https://www.irs.gov/publications/p901, retrieved 2026-09-07): "An individual who is or was immediately before visiting the United States a resident of Germany is exempt from U.S. income tax on **amounts received as a grant, allowance, or award from a nonprofit religious, charitable, scientific, literary, or educational organization**." — a pure university *fellowship* is therefore exempt. But for the RA/TA *wage*: "Individuals described in the previous two paragraphs are also exempt from U.S. income tax on **compensation for dependent personal services of up to $9,000 per year** if: They are present in the United States for **not more than 4 years**; and The services are performed for the purpose of supplementing funds otherwise available for maintenance, education, or training." And the sting: "**If the individual's visit exceeds 4 years, this exemption is lost for the entire visit unless the competent authorities of Germany and the United States agree otherwise.**" A US PhD in oceanography takes five to six years everywhere on this list. **So the practical position is: the first USD 9,000 of assistantship wage is treaty-exempt only if he leaves within four years — which he will not — and the exemption is then clawed back for the whole stay; everything above USD 9,000 is taxable federal income from day one, plus state income tax in RI, NJ, OR, PA and MA (none in TX, WA and FL; CA taxes it).** Budget on roughly USD 3,000–6,000 a year of federal plus state tax on a USD 40,000-ish stipend, and on the treaty being a non-event. He must file a US return every year, including years where treaty exemption is claimed: "income that is not taxable because of an income tax treaty must be reported on a U.S. income tax return even though no income tax is due" (https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-students-and-scholars).

**Practical sequencing for Fall 2027 entry.** The binding constraint is the December 2026 deadline wall — MIT–WHOI 1 Dec, UW 1 Dec, Miami (recruitment weekend) 1 Dec, Scripps 2 Dec, Oregon State 15 Dec, Penn State 15 Dec, Caltech 15 Dec, then Rutgers 1 Jan, URI 1 Jan (funding) / 1 Feb (international), Texas A&M 1 Jan. Before any of those he needs: (1) an **English test booked and sat by October 2026** (TOEFL iBT is the safest single test — it satisfies all nine of the departments that require one; target ≥100 iBT to clear MIT–WHOI's bar, which is the highest); (2) a **GRE General Test** if Caltech geochemistry/geobiology is on the list; (3) **contact with named prospective advisers now** — URI will not admit without a faculty sponsor, Oregon State says mentor availability is "the primary limiting factor", Texas A&M and Miami both say RA funding depends on individual PIs; (4) three referees briefed by October; (5) transcripts from Kocaeli, Bochum (both the 2011 M.Sc. and the 2016–18 doctoral enrolment) and Bremen, with certified English translations and an explanation of each grading scale.
