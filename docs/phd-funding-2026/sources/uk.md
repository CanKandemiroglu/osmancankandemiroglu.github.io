## 0. REGION-LEVEL RULES (verified first, because they gate everything else)

### 0.1 UKRI: are international students eligible?
- url (opened): https://www.ukri.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ESRC-020323-InternationalEligibilityImplementationGuidance-TrainingGrantHolders.pdf — "Changes to EU and International Eligibility for UKRI funded studentships: Implementation Guidance for Training Grant Holders", **issued October 2020, updated March 2021** (document header). Text extracted from the PDF locally.
- verbatim: *"UKRI will be opening up UKRI studentships to international students from AY 2021/22. All students would receive a full award, to include a stipend and fees at the home level. Fees-only awards will no longer be an option. All UKRI studentships must be opened up to both home and international students."*
- verbatim (home definition): *"To be classed as a home student, candidates must meet the following criteria: Be a UK National meeting residency requirement, or Have settled status, or Have pre-settled status meeting residency requirement, or Have indefinite leave to remain or enter"* → a German citizen resident in Germany is **International**.
- Confirmed in the current guidance: https://www.ukri.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/UKRI-290425-AnnotatedRevisionsofUKRITrainingGrantGuidance-Apr25.pdf (UKRI Training Grant Guidance, annotated revisions April 2025), Annex B: *"If a candidate does not meet the criteria above, they would be classed as an International student."*

### 0.2 The 30% cap — is it still 30%, and per what unit?
- **verbatim (the operative sentence):** *"The Cap — The cap will be calculated based on a maximum of 30% of the individuals funded by UKRI **per cohort per training grant** for all standard studentship competitions. Where the 30% does not equal a whole number, this should be rounded up or down to the nearest whole number."* — same PDF as 0.1.
- Also verbatim from the same page: *"There are certain council specific schemes that will not be adopting these standard conditions for example where student numbers are very low or for strategic reasons a lower or higher cap is required. Individual Research Councils will inform their training grant holders where this applies. It will not be possible for institutions to restrict eligibility or impose different cap levels."* and *"All associated studentships are not included in the cap as they are funded in addition to the main award and through a separate recruitment process."*
- **Unit = per cohort, per training grant** (i.e. per DTP/DLA award), **not** per Research Organisation. A university holding two NERC awards gets 30% on each.
- **Still 30% in 2026:** confirmed on a live UKRI page for Industrial CASE, https://www.ukri.org/what-we-do/developing-people-and-skills/how-we-invest-in-research-and-innovation-careers/industrial-doctoral-landscape-awards-idla/industrial-case-training-grants-terms-and-conditions/ — verbatim: *"If there are four or more studentships on the ICASE grant, then the cap of 30% applies. If the ICASE grant comprises one, two or three studentships, a maximum of one international student can be recruited."*
- **Caveat / disagreement between sources:** the current top-level guidance pages do **not** restate the number. https://www.ukri.org/manage-your-award/studentship-guidance/guidance-for-training-grant-holders/ says only *"There is a limit on what percentage of studentships can be awarded to international students in each cohort. This varies between councils."* The UKRI Training Grant Guidance (April 2025) I extracted in full contains **no numeric cap at all** — it refers out to the implementation guidance. So: 30% is the standing NERC/UKRI figure and every NERC DLA page quotes it (see §2), but UKRI's own current guidance leaves the number to the councils.
- **Coordinator's draft wording** ("open to international students with a cap of 30% on international student intake per training grant") is **correct** as to substance and unit. Verified.

### 0.3 Must the institution cover the home/international fee difference?
**No — UKRI does not require it; UKRI only forbids paying it from the grant.**
- url (opened): https://www.ukri.org/publications/terms-and-conditions-for-training-funding/ukri-training-grants-standard-terms-and-conditions-of-training-grant/ — verbatim: *"For International Students, You are able to claim the difference between home Fee and international Fee from other sources, which can include the Student. Training Grant Funds cannot be used to cover the difference between home and overseas fee rates."*
- url (opened): https://www.ukri.org/publications/terms-and-conditions-for-training-funding/ukri-training-grant-guidance/ — verbatim: *"Where you have an international fee that is higher than the home fee, the Standard Conditions allow you to charge the Student the difference between the two."*
- url (opened): https://www.ukri.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/UKRI-290425-AnnotatedRevisionsofUKRITrainingGrantGuidance-Apr25.pdf — verbatim: *"For International Students, You are able to claim the difference between home and International fees from other sources, but these must not be taken from the Training Grant or other UKRI funding. These sources may include an HEI contribution, co-funder contribution or from the student directly. Institutions can also choose to waive the additional fees."*
- 2021 implementation guidance, verbatim: *"For international students, international fees may now be charged, however only the home fee level can be claimed from the UKRI training grant or other UKRI funding. UKRI funding may not be used to cover the difference between the home and international fee rate. Institutions will have the flexibility to find international tuition fees from other sources."*
- **Practical consequence and the single most important filter in this section:** whether a NERC studentship is genuinely *fully funded* for a German citizen depends entirely on the host consortium's local policy. §2 records this per award. Where the difference is **not** waived, the shortfall is roughly **£22,000–£28,000 per year** (Bristol Earth Sciences overseas fee is £27,500/yr vs home £5,106 — https://www.bristol.ac.uk/study/postgraduate/research/earth-sciences/). Those are **not fully funded**.

## C. How PhD funding works in the UK (and what it means for this candidate)

**1. Stipend, not salary.** A UK PhD studentship is a *studentship*, not employment: there is no contract of employment, no employer social insurance, no pension, and no unemployment cover. The stipend is **tax-free and non-repayable** — UKRI's 2026/27 minimum is **GBP 21,805 p.a. (GBP 23,805 in London)**, paid monthly, i.e. roughly **£1,817/month net outside London** (source: https://www.ukri.org/apply-for-funding/studentships-and-doctoral-training/get-a-studentship-to-fund-your-doctorate/). That is the *entire* income; there is nothing above it. Compare this with a German TV-L 65–75% doctoral contract, which is gross salary with pension and social insurance — the UK figure is net but structurally poorer in benefits.

**2. Fees are the whole game for a non-UK citizen.** UKRI pays fees only at the **home rate (£5,238 for 2026/27)**. The overseas rate at a research university is roughly **£27,500/yr** (Bristol Earth Sciences). UKRI explicitly permits the university to bill the student the difference (§0.3). So the decisive question for every NERC studentship is not "am I eligible?" (he is) but **"who pays the £22,000/yr gap?"**. From the pages opened: **GW4+, ARIES, YES•DTN, E5, ACCE+ and QUARTILES waive or cover it; CENTA3 explicitly does not; IAPETUS leaves it to the institution; IGNITE does not say.** Never apply to a NERC project without a written answer to that question.

**3. The 30% cap is real and it binds.** It is *"a maximum of 30% of the individuals funded by UKRI per cohort per training grant"*. QUARTILES had already exhausted its international quota for October 2026 by the time of this research. In practice a German applicant is competing for roughly 5 places per DLA per year against a global field, whereas home applicants compete for ~14.

**4. Costs no studentship covers.** Student visa **GBP 558** (from outside the UK; also £558 to extend or switch from inside), plus the **Immigration Health Surcharge at GBP 776 per year for students** (£1,035/yr for other routes), paid **up-front for the whole visa length** — and gov.uk warns *"A visa may last longer than your course of study"*. For a 3.5-year PhD the visa is normally granted for course length plus 4 months, so budget roughly **£3,100 IHS + £558 visa ≈ £3,700 before arrival** (that arithmetic is mine; the per-year rates are quoted). Sources: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa and https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay. Almost every DTP page opened says so explicitly (ARIES, ACCE+, QUARTILES, YES•DTN, GW4+). **Note:** gov.uk also publishes an IHS *refund* route for students from the EU/EEA/Switzerland (https://www.gov.uk/apply-student-immigration-health-surcharge-refund and https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-health-surcharge-for-eu-students-in-the-uk) — as a German citizen he should investigate whether he qualifies, but I did not open those pages in detail.

**5. Where the money actually is for a German citizen.** The schemes that pay the **full overseas fee plus a stipend** are the university-funded ones, not the research-council ones: **Imperial President's PhD (£26,500/yr — the highest found), Clarendon at Oxford, Gates Cambridge and Harding at Cambridge, Edinburgh Doctoral College Scholarships, Bristol PGR Scholarships, and Plymouth SERVO.** These have **no nationality cap at all**. Strategically, the university scholarships are the stronger play and the NERC DLAs are the higher-volume but capped play; the strongest applications pursue both in parallel, because both route through the same departmental supervisor relationship.

## 5. England-only vs UK, and nation-restricted schemes

- **No NERC Doctoral Landscape Award found is restricted by UK nation as regards the applicant.** DLAs are located in a nation (E5 and half of IAPETUS in Scotland; QUARTILES in Scotland/NI; GW4+ spans England and Wales) but they recruit internationally within the 30% cap. There is no "England-only" NERC studentship.
- **The "England" in UKRI's rules is a legal-definition artefact, not a geographic restriction.** UKRI determines home/international status by reference to *"the Education (Fees and Awards) (England) Regulations 2007"* and applies that test UK-wide — verbatim from the UKRI Training Grant Guidance (April 2025): *"Research Organisations should determine the status of a student by reviewing the Education (Fees and Awards) (England) regulations 2007 in the usual way, noting that UKRI determine eligibility based on the first day of the studentship, rather than the first day of the academic year."* UKRI also notes it has power *"under Reg. 64 of the Education (Fees & Awards) (England) Regulations 2007 to take a wider approach"* than the DfE. So a Scottish or Welsh host applies the same England-regulation test.
- **Genuinely nation-restricted things do exist but are not relevant here:** SGSAH (Scottish Graduate School for Arts & Humanities) and the Welsh-government-funded schemes are humanities/social-science and/or nationality-restricted; neither covers marine geoscience. Not researched further.
- **One route found that is restricted by fee status rather than nation:** CENTA's CSOS scholarships — *"international students are not eligible to apply via this route"* (https://centa.ac.uk/apply/).
