This means they may ask you to do a different test.'" Otherwise applicants must "pass a Secure English Language Test (SELT) from an approved provider" demonstrating the required CEFR level.
- **This confirms the brief's expectation**: for a PhD (a degree-level course), the sponsoring university itself typically assesses English (which is exactly what all 14 institutions above do, via their own accepted-test lists and, in several cases, an explicit "Higher Education Provider" self-assessment route) — so a **standard (non-UKVI) IELTS Academic**, or one of the other university-accepted tests, is normally sufficient for the visa step; a UKVI-badged SELT specifically is not automatically mandatory for the visa route as long as the sponsoring HEP's own English assessment is satisfied. Several universities above (Southampton, Curtin, UNSW) explicitly also accept plain "IELTS Academic" or "IELTS Academic/UKVI" interchangeably for admission purposes, reinforcing this.
- Majority-English-speaking-country list on this same gov.uk page (exempted from the Knowledge of English requirement altogether): "Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, the Bahamas, Barbados, Belize, the British overseas territories, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Ireland, Jamaica, Malta, New Zealand, St Kitts and Nevis, St Lucia, St Vincent and the Grenadines, Trinidad and Tobago, UK, USA," plus "Canadian nationals" and "US students in study abroad programmes" as additional exemptions. **Germany is not on this list.**
- This gov.uk finding is corroborated by every non-UK university list opened above (US/Canada/Australia/NZ), none of which name Germany as a majority-English-speaking or automatically-exempt country either.
