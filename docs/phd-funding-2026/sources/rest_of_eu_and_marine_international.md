# C. Notes on how funding works in this region

**The single most important structural fact: outside the Anglosphere, a European PhD is usually a job, not a scholarship.** In the Netherlands, Norway, Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Switzerland, Austria, France and Germany the doctoral candidate signs an **employment contract**, is paid a **taxable gross salary** with pension and social-insurance cover, and pays **no tuition fee** — so the "fully funded" test is met by construction, and there is no separate scholarship round, no personal proposal and, critically, **no rule anywhere in these systems that asks about prior incomplete doctoral enrolment**. He applies to advertised vacancies exactly as he would to any job. Verified 2026 gross figures across the region: Netherlands **EUR 3,059–3,881/month** on scale P (https://www.staff.universiteitleiden.nl/human-resources/conditions-of-employment-salary-and-allowance/salary/salary-scales-and-periodic-rise, 1 July 2025), with live vacancies at EUR 3,204–4,051 and EUR 3,463; Norway **NOK 568,700/year** (https://www.jobbnorge.no/en/available-jobs/joblisting/pdf/293286); Denmark **DKK 37,313.75/month including pension** at entry (https://phd.arts.au.dk/fileadmin/phd.arts.au.dk/ARTS/Rules_and_forms/Loenoversigt_UK.pdf, as of 1 August 2026); Sweden **SEK 32,900/month** from 1 October 2026 (https://medarbetare.su.se/en/employment/my-employment/salary-and-compensation/salary-for-doctoral-students); Finland **EUR 2,500–2,800/month** (https://jobs.helsinki.fi/job/Helsinki-University-funded-doctoral-researcher-positions/1370097857/); Switzerland **CHF 55,225/year** at EPFL (https://www.epfl.ch/campus/services/human-resources/basic-starting-salary-of-doctoral-assistants-and-postdocs); Austria **EUR 50,220/year at the funded 30h/week level, EUR 2,832.10/month** (https://www.fwf.ac.at/en/funding/steps-to-your-fwf-project/further-information/personnel-costs); France **EUR 2,300 gross/month** from 1 January 2026 by ministerial arrêté (https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000046820745).

**The southern and western-fringe systems are different and this is where his history matters.** Belgium (FWO), Portugal (FCT), Ireland (Research Ireland) and Italy award **fellowships/studentships** through competitive annual rounds with personal proposals — and it is precisely these that carry seniority caps and prior-funding clauses. FWO's **"maximum of 18 months of scientific seniority"** plus **"The time span between your Master's degree or equivalent and 01 March 2026 may not be longer than three years"** is the sharpest hazard in this whole report given his 2011 master's and his 2016–2018 Bochum enrolment. FCT's clause is milder and he passes it: it bars only those who have previously received *FCT* PhD funding or who already hold a doctorate — **but FCT pays only EUR 1,309.64/month (2025 table), a bolsa rather than a salary, with no pension accrual.** Research Ireland's rule is a **"new entrants … or … registered students in the first year"** test which he should pass but should disclose against. Italian and Spanish rules on prior *funded* doctoral places could not be verified from primary pages here.

**The marine- and polar-specific international bodies do not fund PhDs at all.** Verified: POGO and its Nippon Foundation schemes are explicitly restricted to developing-country nationals ("Applicants must also be citizens of developing countries or economies in transition"); IOC-UNESCO offers training courses with no doctoral funding on any page I could open; ECORD offers a **EUR 4,000 maximum** research/travel grant and summer-school attendance scholarships, both of which *require you to already be a PhD student*; the Antarctic Science International Bursary averages roughly **GBP 4,670** per award, is paid to the institution, and funds research costs, not living costs; SCAR fellowships (USD 15,000) require you to be a "Current PhD researcher". Every one of these is a **top-up for someone already enrolled** — useful in year 1–2, useless as a way in.

**Two practical filters worth acting on.** First, the **MSCA mobility rule cuts the wrong way inside Germany and the right way everywhere else**: "must not have resided or carried out your main activity … in [country] for more than 12 months in the three years prior to the contract start date" means German-hosted MSCA-DN posts are closed to him while every non-German MSCA-DN post in Europe is automatically open — a large, under-exploited pool. Second, **the absence of IELTS/TOEFL is a blocker only in the UK and Australia**, not in the continental employment systems, where advertised posts ask for "good command of spoken and written English" with no certificate; the one continental exception found was UiB, whose advert asks for "approved documentation of proficiency in English (if required, cf. English language requirements for PhD admission)".
