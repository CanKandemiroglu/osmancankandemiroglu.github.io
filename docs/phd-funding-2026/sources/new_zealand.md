## 0. THE KEY QUESTION — do international PhD students pay DOMESTIC tuition fees in NZ?

**YES — confirmed on university pages opened 2026-09-07, conditional on physically residing in New Zealand.** I could not find an Education New Zealand or TEC/government page that I could open stating the policy; the verbatim statements below come from two universities' own 2026 fee pages, which is the operative level anyway (the universities administer the scheme).

**University of Auckland — verbatim** (https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/study/fees-and-money-matters/tuition-fees/doctoral-fees.html, opened 2026-09-07; no page date shown):
> "If you are a new international PhD student, you will pay the same annual tuition fee as New Zealand PhD students. The 2026 PhD tuition fee is NZ$8,847.60 per annum."
> "To be eligible for the domestic PhD fee, you must reside in New Zealand for the duration of your doctoral programme. However, you may undertake research activities overseas during your doctoral programme for a cumulative total of no more than 12 months."
> "If you lose your domestic eligibility, you will pay international fees."
Plus, same page: "Student Services Fee of $9.44 per point, estimated at $1,132.80 for full time study (120 points) in 2026".

**CONFIRMS THE DRAFT FIGURE: the Auckland 2026 domestic doctoral fee is NZ$8,847.60 per annum** (official page, opened). Note that the Student Services Fee of ~NZ$1,132.80 (2026) sits on top.

Second Auckland source (student FAQ, opened): https://uoa.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/8907/~/domestic-fee-policy-for-international-candidates-and-nz-permanent-residents — "If you are an international student enrolled into a PhD programme, you will only pay domestic fees for the duration of your study."

**University of Waikato — verbatim** (https://www.waikato.ac.nz/int/study/fees-costs/international-tuition-costs/, opened 2026-09-07):
> "New international PhD students, studying full-time, may be eligible to pay domestic New Zealand annual tuition fees."
> "A student enrolled in a New Zealand university but remaining resident overseas does not have domestic student status."
2026 Waikato domestic doctoral fee range on that page: NZ$7,567 – NZ$8,037 per year depending on subject; overseas research absence capped at "no more than 12 months in total during the period of their candidature".

**Victoria University of Wellington — verbatim** (https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/fgr/apply/funding, opened 2026-09-07):
> international PhD students "may be eligible to pay the same 'domestic' fees as PhD students who are citizens, residents, or permanent residents of New Zealand."

### What this does to the funding maths
Tuition is NOT the obstacle in New Zealand. An unfunded international PhD costs roughly NZ$7,600–8,900/yr in tuition + ~NZ$1,000–1,150 student services fee + compulsory health insurance — i.e. the fee gap that dominates the UK/Australia/US calculation essentially disappears. **The whole question in NZ is therefore the living stipend, not the fee waiver.** A "fees-only" NZ scholarship is worth little (~NZ$8k); a stipend-only award still leaves only a small fee bill. Conversely, "fully funded" in NZ means stipend + domestic fees, and the residual uncovered items are usually the Student Services Fee, medical/travel insurance, and relocation. Critically, the domestic-fee status is **conditional on living in NZ** — a write-up year spent in Germany breaks it and reverts him to international fees.

### Work rights — partner
https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-student-work-visa/ (opened 2026-09-07). Verbatim:
> Your partner must be studying "a level 9 or 10 qualification on the" NZQCF, or "a level 7 or 8 qualification specified for a role on our Green List".
> "can do any work, in any job, anywhere in New Zealand"
> visa granted for "the same duration as your partner's student visa"
> Cost: "NZD $1630".
A PhD is NZQCF **level 10**, so the partner of a PhD student qualifies for an **open work visa** — this is one of NZ's strongest structural advantages over the UK (which removed dependant rights for most students) and Germany. (I also opened https://www.immigration.govt.nz/about-us/news-centre/update-to-work-rights-for-partners-of-a-student/ — published 21 June 2024 — but it deals only with Green List bachelor's degrees and says nothing about doctorates; and https://immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/72353.htm which covers only NZ Aid Programme students. The level 9/10 rule above is the operative one.)

### Post-study work visa — PhD graduates
https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/post-study-work-visa/ (opened 2026-09-07). Verbatim:
> qualifies with "a level 10 doctoral degree"; may stay and work "for up to 3 years depending on your study"
> "If you studied towards a Doctoral degree, you can apply for a Post-Study Work Visa after submitting your thesis."
> apply "no later than 6 months after the end date of your student visa if your qualification is a Doctoral degree"
> cost "From NZD $1670"; funds "at least NZD $5,000 in total for your stay"
> work rights: "do any work in any job, anywhere in New Zealand"
**3 years of open post-PhD work rights, obtainable at thesis submission (not at graduation).** This materially improves the post-doc landing.

## C. Notes on how funding works in this region

**Structure.** New Zealand PhD funding is a **stipend + fee-waiver scholarship model, not an employment contract.** There is no NZ equivalent of a German TV-L 65% position or an MSCA salary: the recipient is a student, not an employee, gets no employer pension or social-insurance contributions, and no unemployment cover. All five university awards above are granted as part of the doctoral *admission* decision — there is no separate application form at Auckland, VUW, Canterbury or Otago, and at Waikato an Offer of Place must already be in hand at the closing date. Because the government subsidises international PhD tuition down to the domestic rate (section 0), a NZ "fully funded" package is cheap for the university to assemble, which is why stipends are the binding constraint and why the offer rate is comparatively generous — but it also means the residual out-of-pocket items (health insurance, Student Services Fee, relocation from Germany, visa fees) are real. Only Auckland covers the international student's health insurance charge; Waikato states outright that "medical insurance, moving costs, and research expenses must be met by other means", and AskOtago says "Many scholarships do not cover the Student Services Fee, insurance (for international students), or other sundry fees".

**Tax.** Doctoral stipends are treated as **tax-free**. The University of Auckland describes its award as "a tax-free stipend of up to $35,468 pa" (https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/study/scholarships-and-awards/scholarship-types/scholarships-for-international-students/doctoral-scholarships-for-international-students.html, opened). The statutory basis is s CW 36 of the Income Tax Act 2007, interpreted in Inland Revenue's IS 15/01 (issued 28 August 2015; https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/interpretation-statements/is-1501-income-tax-tax-exempt-scholarships-and-bursaries-s-cw-36 and the PDF at https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is1501.pdf, both opened; PDF text extracted). Verbatim from IS 15/01: "Certain scholarships and bursaries are exempt income under s CW 36."; "For the exemption to apply, the scholarship or bursary must be made for the dominant purpose of assisting with the recipient's education."; and the courts weigh "the relationship between the payer and the payee; the contractual basis for the payment; the conditions of the agreement; and anything the payee is required to provide in return for the payment." **Caveat worth flagging:** the exemption turns on the payment being *for education*, not for services — so a stipend restructured as salaried research assistance would be taxable, and IS 15/01 devotes "Example 3 - Employment income rather than a scholarship or bursary" to exactly that case. (I could not open https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1513255.html — HTTP 403 — so the statute text itself is cited only via IRD's own interpretation statement.)

**Typical 2026 stipend and the living-cost gap.** Verified 2026 full-time doctoral stipends: **Auckland NZ$35,468** (42 months, + fees + SSF + single-student health insurance); **Waikato NZ$33,000** (36 months, + domestic fees + SSF, insurance excluded); **Canterbury NZ$32,650 per 120 points** (up to 360 points, + thesis-only domestic tuition); **VUW NZ$32,000** (36 months, + tuition, insurance and levies excluded); **Otago reported NZ$34,128 but NOT VERIFIED** (host blocked). So the tax-free band is roughly **NZ$32,000–35,500 per year, i.e. about NZ$2,670–2,960 per month net**, with no tax and no social-insurance deductions. **I was unable to verify an official cost-of-living figure for Auckland, Wellington or Dunedin from a page I could open in this pass** (the web-search budget was exhausted), so I will not put a number on the gap. What can be said from verified figures alone: the stipends are pegged near a level that assumes shared accommodation, and the ranking of pressure is Auckland > Wellington > Dunedin/Hamilton/Christchurch — which cuts against Auckland's nominally highest stipend and in favour of Otago (Dunedin), Waikato (Hamilton/Tauranga) and Canterbury (Christchurch) in real terms. Two real offsets exist and are verified: the **partner's open work visa** (level 10 study, section 0) can add a second full income, and the scholarships permit paid work of 750 h/yr (Auckland), 600 h/yr (VUW) or 500 h/yr (Waikato).

**The single decisive practical obstacle: English evidence.** He has UNIcert III and no IELTS/TOEFL. UNIcert appears on none of the accepted-test lists at Auckland, VUW or Otago. Auckland's automatic exemption covers only citizens of Canada, Ireland, Singapore, the UK and the USA; VUW's List A/List B mechanism does not appear to include Germany and the Faculty of Graduate Research states the requirements are "strict and are not negotiable"; only Otago's wording ("Previous tertiary study completed in English may be accepted…") leaves room for an argument, and a German-taught Bremen M.Sc. with an English thesis is a weak fit for it. **Booking IELTS Academic (target 7.0+ overall, no band below 6.5, to clear all five universities and the Otago 7.0-per-band bar reported for some programmes) is the highest-priority, longest-lead action and gates every NZ application.** Test slots plus results take roughly 4–6 weeks; the next actionable deadlines are Waikato 31 October 2026, VUW 1 November 2026, Auckland 1 November 2026, Canterbury 15 October 2026 — so the test must be sat by about mid-September 2026 to make the October–November round, otherwise the target becomes Auckland 1 February 2027 / VUW 1 March 2027.
