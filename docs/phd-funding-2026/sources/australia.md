## 0. THE FEDERAL LAYER: Research Training Program (RTP) — this is the answer to the "prior doctoral enrolment" question

**What the RTP is and what it contains — verbatim, Department of Education**
(https://www.education.gov.au/research-block-grants/research-training-program):

> "Students can be offered RTP scholarships for one or more of the following:
> *   tuition fees offset
> *   a stipend for general living costs
> *   allowances related to the ancillary cost of HDRs."

> "The Australian Government provides funding to eligible HEPs through the RTP to support their
> training of domestic and overseas students undertaking research doctorate and research masters
> degrees."

**National RTP stipend rates — verbatim table, same page:**

| Year | Full-time base RTP stipend rate ($) | Full-time max RTP stipend rate ($) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027 | 35,550 | 55,537 |
| 2026 | 34,315 | 53,608 |
| 2025 | 33,511 | 52,352 |

> "The RTP base and maximum stipend rates are indexed each year, effective from 1 January. The base
> and maximum RTP stipend rates for the following year are announced by 1 October each year."

So: **2026 national base = AUD 34,315/yr; 2027 national base = AUD 35,550/yr.** Universities may pay
anywhere between base and max, and most Go8 universities pay well above base (see section A).

**International eligibility — verbatim, Department of Education RTP FAQs for students, FAQ 28**
(https://www.education.gov.au/research-block-grants/research-training-program/research-training-program-frequently-asked-questions-students):

> "### 28. Can I apply for an RTP scholarship if I am an international student?
> To be eligible for an RTP Scholarship (whether RTP Stipend, RTP Fees Offset, or RTP Allowance), a
> student must be a domestic student or an international student enrolled in an accredited research
> doctorate or research masters degree at an eligible Australian university.
> In administering RTP scholarships, universities may only spend up to 10% of their RTP funding per
> grant year on providing scholarships to international students."

**The legislated eligibility test — verbatim, Higher Education Support (Commonwealth Scholarships)
Guidelines 2025 (F2025L01474, in force 2 December 2025), s.14**
(https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01474/asmade/2025-12-02/text/original/word — the Word download
linked from https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01474/asmade/downloads):

> "**14  Student eligibility requirements**
> A student must meet the following requirements to be eligible for scholarships under this Part:
> (a) the student must be a domestic student or an overseas student enrolled in an accredited HDR
> course of study at a HEP;
> (b) in relation to RTP Fees Offset, the student must not be receiving an equivalent award or
> scholarship from the Commonwealth designed to offset HDR fees."

**That is the entire Commonwealth eligibility test. There is NO federal exclusion for prior doctoral
enrolment, prior incomplete doctorate, or prior RTP support.** What exists federally is only a
*permission* for universities to prioritise first-time HDR students — verbatim, Guidelines 2025,
Note to s.18(1):

> "Note: A HEP may wish to give priority to a class of students, such as Indigenous students, low
> socioeconomic status (SES) students, students undertaking research in a particular discipline or
> emerging area of research strength, or students undertaking a HDR for the first time, as part of
> their competitive selection process."

**The Department answers the candidate's exact question three times — verbatim, RTP FAQs for students:**

> "### 9. I hold a research degree from an overseas university. Am I eligible for an RTP scholarship?
> The Guidelines do not restrict students who hold a research degree from an overseas university
> from eligibility for an RTP scholarship."

> "### 10. Am I eligible for an RTP scholarship if I have previously completed a HDR?
> The Guidelines do not restrict students who have previously completed a higher degrees by research
> (HDR) from eligibility for an RTP scholarship."

> "### 13. I have received an RTP scholarship in the past but withdrew from the degree prior to
> completion. Can I apply for another RTP scholarship?
> The Guidelines do not prevent you from being awarded an RTP scholarship for an additional degree if
> you have previously received an RTP scholarship. …
> Universities may also wish to give priority to a certain class of students such as … students
> undertaking higher degree by research (HDR) study for the first time."

**Does a prior non-Australian doctoral enrolment that ended without a degree count against him?**
- **Federally: no.** He never *held* a Research Doctorate; s.14 imposes no bar; FAQ 9/10/13 confirm.
- **At university level: it depends, and the barred cases are all about a COMPLETED doctorate,
  which he does not have.** Every university rule I opened that excludes anyone excludes people who
  *hold / have completed* a research doctorate — see the verbatim quotes under Curtin, JCU, UWA,
  Sydney and Flinders in sections A/B. He passes all of those on their literal wording.
- **The residual risk is the "first-time HDR" preference**, which several universities apply as a
  *ranking* factor (UWA, JCU, Sydney, Curtin — quoted below). A 2016–2018 doctoral enrolment at
  Ruhr-Universität Bochum that ended without a degree is very likely to be read as "not enrolling in
  a HDR for the first time" and can cost ranking points in a competitive round. It is not a
  disqualifier anywhere I found.
- **It must be disclosed.** UWA's 2027 application instructions require, verbatim: "Final official
  academic transcripts (including grading scale) and degree certificates for all tertiary education
  **(including incomplete degrees)**." Concealing the 2016–18 enrolment would be a misconduct ground
  (UWA ITFOS Conditions cl.14.1(iii): termination if the candidate "is found to have committed
  serious misconduct, including providing false or misleading information").

**The real scarcity, stated in the law itself — Guidelines 2025 s.15(2), verbatim:**

> "(2) A HEP must only spend up to a maximum of 10 per cent of its RTP Grant Amount in a Grant Year
> on the provision of RTP Scholarships to overseas students."

and s.17(3), verbatim:

> "(3) A HEP must only award an RTP Fees Offset to an overseas student as a result of a competitive
> selection process, with the nature of the competitive selection process to be determined by the HEP."

This 10% cap is the single most important structural fact for this candidate: the stipend is
plentiful, the **international fees offset is the scarce component** (see section C).

# C. How PhD funding works in Australia (and where the real bottleneck is)

**1. It is a stipend, not an employment contract.** Australian PhD funding is a *scholarship*, not a salary: there is no TV-L-style contract, no employer social-security contributions, no pension accrual, no unemployment insurance. The full-time stipend is **income-tax-exempt** — verbatim from UTAS (https://www.utas.edu.au/research/degrees/scholarships-and-fees): "Please note: Full-time scholarships are considered by the **Australian Taxation Office to be exempt income, and are therefore not taxed**. Part-time scholarships are, however, deemed taxable income and taxed." The UTAS TGRS Conditions of Award 2026 say the same in figures: "Full-time candidates receive a stipend at the rate of AUD$34,315pa **non-taxable**"; "Part-time candidates receive a stipend at the rate of AUD$17,158pa **taxable**"; and "Under Australian Taxation Law part-time stipends are taxable." Curtin's conditions likewise: the stipend "**is exempt from taxation**." (I could not open the ATO's own scholarship page — https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/income-you-must-declare/other-income/scholarship-bursary-grant-and-award-payments rendered as navigation only — so these three university statements are the cited authority. **Never go part-time: it halves the money and makes it taxable.**)

**2. A funded international PhD is three separate awards that must all land together.** (a) an **RTP/university Stipend** for living costs, (b) an **RTP Fees Offset / International Tuition Fee Scholarship** to extinguish the international tuition fee, and (c) **OSHC**, the health insurance the student visa (subclass 500) legally requires. Some universities bundle all three (UNSW RTPI, Sydney RTP Fee Offset, Melbourne RTP Stipend+Fee Offset, ANU RTP International, UWA RTP Fees Offset/UIFS, JCU IRTP). Others deliberately do not: **UTAS excludes OSHC from both RTP and TGRS** ("does not cover … Overseas Health Cover, and visa costs"), and JCU's Postgraduate Research Scholarship and AIMS@JCU Scholarship both list "Overseas health cover" under Exclusions. **A stipend without a fee offset is not funding at all for an international** — international HDR tuition at these universities runs roughly AUD 35,000–50,000 per year, which would swallow the entire stipend.

**3. Typical 2026/2027 full-time stipend range, from the pages opened:** Commonwealth base **AUD 34,315 (2026) / AUD 35,550 (2027)**; Commonwealth maximum **AUD 53,608 (2026) / AUD 55,537 (2027)** but the maximum is in practice reserved for Indigenous RTP awards. Actual advertised rates: UTAS **34,315** (2026) · UNSW **39,206** (2026) · UWA **38,110** (2026) → **39,253.30** (2027 international round) · UniMelb **39,500** (2026) · JCU **37,500** (2027) · ANU **40,475** (2027) · Sydney **42,754** (2026) → **44,293** (2027). So the realistic band is **AUD 34,000–44,500 p.a., tax-free**, i.e. roughly **AUD 2,860–3,700 per month tax-free**. Against Australian big-city rents that is comfortable in Hobart and thin in Sydney. Add: relocation allowances of AUD 2,000–3,000 (UTAS 2,000; ANU 2,500 international; UniMelb 3,000 from outside Australia), thesis allowances (ANU up to AUD 500), and 20 days' paid recreation leave + 10 days' personal leave per year, now legislated federally (Guidelines 2025 s.18(6)).

**4. The scarce component is the international fees offset, and the law says exactly how scarce.** Guidelines 2025 s.15(2): "A HEP must only spend up to a **maximum of 10 per cent** of its RTP Grant Amount in a Grant Year on the provision of RTP Scholarships to overseas students." Combined with s.17(3), which forces every international fees offset through "a competitive selection process", this means an international applicant is competing for roughly a tenth of each university's research-training money. Domestic students get the fee offset automatically ("The RTP Fees Offset covers tuition fees for domestic candidates … The Australian Government currently provides funding to all Australian universities to cover the cost of research degree places", UTAS); internationals get it only by winning a ranked round. **This — not the prior-doctorate question — is the actual obstacle.** The practical countermeasures are: (i) apply to the round that bundles fees+stipend+OSHC as one award (UNSW RTPI, Sydney RTP, Melbourne RTP Stipend+Fee Offset, UWA, ANU); (ii) go for an **advertised project with a named supervisor holding grant money**, which raises ranking; (iii) apply to more than one university in the same cycle — the Department explicitly permits it ("You can apply for an RTP scholarship at more than one university, or more than once at the same university", FAQ 4).

**5. Visa and the money you must show anyway.** The student visa (subclass 500) requires OSHC for the whole stay and evidence of funds; UTAS puts it plainly: "The Department of Home Affairs requires evidence that applicants for a student visa have sufficient funds to complete their studies. You need to consider the following expenses: * Course tuition fees * Visa and medical (pre-departure) fees * General living expenses * **Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** * Return airfares". Published single-cover OSHC quotes: UTAS **AUD 4,467** for a 60-month PhD; JCU **AUD 4,702** (2026, "one-off upfront payment"), rising for 2027. There is also a Student Services and Amenities Fee (SSAF) that **no** scholarship in this report covers.

**6. The one universal blocker for this candidate: English.** Every university checked requires a *test* or a narrowly-defined exemption, and **none of them lists UNIcert III**. UTAS: IELTS 7.0 (no band <6.5) / TOEFL iBT 94 / PTE 65 / Cambridge C1 Advanced grade C, "awarded within 2 years of application date", or a graduate degree "where the medium and language of instruction of the degree was **entirely in English**". UWA (HDR standard): IELTS 6.5 (6.0 each) / TOEFL iBT 82 / PTE 64 / Cambridge CAE grade B, within two years. JCU: "ELR Band 2". UNSW: test results, or "Prior study in the medium of English", demonstrated "**before the scholarship deadline**". A German-taught M.Sc. with an English thesis will not satisfy "entirely in English". **Booking IELTS Academic (target 7.0 overall, no band below 6.5, which clears every institution here) is the single highest-value action, and it must be done before any of the 2027 deadlines.**
