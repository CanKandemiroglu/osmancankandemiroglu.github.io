Instant Visualization of Organic Matter Transformation in the Ocean
MARUM (Hinrichs Lab) · MPI for Marine Microbiology, Marine Glycobiology · BIOM (Hehemann Lab) · defended 23.03.2026 · DFG TRR 420 CONCENTRATE · Guest Scientist, AG Hehemann; Research Student, MPI Bremen
  • Designed and ran five particle-degradation experiments (ADE I–V) on six marine Vibrio strains — mono- and co-culture, biological triplicates, incubations to 590 h — tracking 302–353 individually matched particles per run. Broadcasters removed 78.1 ± 17.6 % of particle volume against aggregators’ 50.5 ± 24.2 % (t = 7.03, P = 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰).
  • Phosphate acts as a specific metabolic switch: at 0.2 mM the broadcaster phenotype removed 2.1× more particle volume (median 82.5 % vs 39.3 %; U = 959, P = 0.0005); the advantage vanishes under depletion, and in co-culture the 2.2× advantage collapses to 1.05× — traced to one alginate-lyase gene (PL7G), not the phenotype. Built the system from scratch: calcium-alginate microparticles (124.0 ± 49.1 µm) and a custom Marine Minimal Medium family isolating one nutrient per run.
  • Built the particle-imaging-to-rate pipeline in Python 3.11: time-lapse microscopy → ImageJ Feret/ESD sizing → particle volume → daily carbon degradation rate → carbon loss per metre, with Hungarian-algorithm matching — a chain that transfers directly to in-situ particle-camera imagery. A settling model on the measured rates predicts Martin exponents of 0.95 vs 0.78 (~11 % vs ~17 % of particle carbon reaching 1,000 m).

¹³C stable-isotope probing and diffusive-flux geochemistry — Helgoland Mud Area, North Sea
Biogeochemistry Projects, MARUM / AWI · core HE595 GC-11, 452 cm · support M. Elvert, V. Heuer, L. Wörmer; porewater section reviewed by Prof. Dr. S. Kasten
  • Designed and ran ¹³C-acetate incubations in three arms (48 h/28 °C, 120 h/28 °C, 120 h/8 °C, frozen t₀) on four depths spanning the sulfate-reduction, sulfate–methane-transition and methanogenic zones, anaerobic throughout. Traced the label into individual fatty acids by GC-C-IRMS (C16:1ω7, C16:1ω5): bacterial communities respond to elevated temperature faster than archaeal, and the OH-archaeol/archaeol ratio resolves an ANME-1 → ANME-2 shift across the transition.
  • Extracted porewater by rhizone sampling (0.1 µm), measured nutrients and DIC on a QuAAtro39, and converted the sulfate gradient into a diffusive flux by Fick’s law with tortuosity correction (φ = 0.6, θ² = 2.02): J = −0.219 mol m⁻² yr⁻¹. Documented the redox cascade after oxygen exposure of reduced sediment — sulfide re-oxidation, Fe and Mn re-precipitation, phosphate re-adsorption onto fresh oxides.
